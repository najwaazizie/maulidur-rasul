MAULIDUR RASUL · THE PROPHET'S BIRTHDAY

The Life of the Messenger ﷺ Muhammad, may peace be upon him

From an orphaned boy raised in the hills of Mecca to a mercy the world still turns to — walk through every chapter of his story, moment by moment.

570 CE Mecca 632 CE Madinah

Why we mark Maulidur Rasul

Maulidur Rasul is the day Muslims remember the birth of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ and reflect on his life — his honesty, patience, and compassion. It's less about celebration for its own sake, and more about revisiting his story so his example can guide how we live. Scroll through his life below, or jump straight to a period that interests you.

Birth
570 CE — Year of the Elephant

Born in Mecca

Muhammad ﷺ was born in Mecca into the Banu Hashim clan of the Quraysh tribe. His father, Abdullah, had passed away before he was born, so from his very first day, he grew up without a father.

That same year, an army from Yemen marched on Mecca with war elephants, intending to destroy the Kaaba — the attack failed, and the year became known ever after as "the Year of the Elephant."
Family
Age 6 – 8

Early losses, a caring family

His mother Aminah passed away when he was six, and his grandfather Abdul Muttalib, who then cared for him, died two years later. His uncle Abu Talib took him in and raised him with love for the rest of his childhood.

As a baby, he was sent to be nursed in the desert by Halimah al-Sa'diyah, a common Meccan custom believed to build stronger health and clearer Arabic — a practice thought to have shaped his calm, eloquent manner.
Character
Childhood & youth

"Al-Amin" — The Trustworthy

As a young shepherd and later a trader travelling with caravans, he became known across Mecca for his honesty and fairness. People called him Al-Amin, "the trustworthy one," and often asked him to settle disputes.

At around 35, rival clans nearly came to blows over who would place the sacred Black Stone back into the rebuilt Kaaba wall. His solution — having every clan lift it together on a cloth — avoided a war entirely.
Marriage
Age 25

Marriage to Khadijah

He married Khadijah bint Khuwaylid, a respected and successful businesswoman fifteen years his senior. Their marriage was one of deep partnership, and she became the very first person to believe in his message.

Khadijah proposed to him through an intermediary after being deeply impressed by his honesty while trading her goods. They stayed married for about 25 years, until her passing.
Revelation
Age 40 · 610 CE

The first revelation

While reflecting alone in the Cave of Hira, he received his first revelation from the Angel Jibril (Gabriel) — the opening verses of Surah Al-Alaq. This marked the beginning of his mission as a Prophet.

Shaken by the encounter, he hurried home and asked Khadijah to wrap him in a blanket. It was her calm reassurance that helped him understand what had just happened to him.
Growing faith
Years 1 – 10 of Prophethood

Preaching in Mecca

He first shared the message quietly with close family and friends, then openly with the whole city. Many early Muslims faced harsh persecution for following him — yet the small community held firm and slowly grew.

Early believers came from every level of society — from Khadijah and young Ali, to Bilal, a formerly enslaved man who endured torture for his faith and later became the first person to call the adhan.
Trial
Year 10 of Prophethood

The Year of Sorrow

Known as Aam al-Huzn, this was one of the hardest years of his life: he lost both Khadijah, his beloved wife, and Abu Talib, his protective uncle, within a short span of time.

In that same painful year, he travelled to the nearby town of Ta'if hoping for support — instead he was mocked and stoned by its people, one of the most difficult days described in his life.
Miracle
Year 11 – 12 of Prophethood

Isra' and Mi'raj — the Night Journey

In one night, he was miraculously taken from Mecca to Jerusalem and then ascended through the heavens. It was during this journey that the five daily prayers were prescribed to Muslims.

When he described the journey to a skeptical crowd the next morning, his close friend Abu Bakr believed him instantly and without question — earning him the title "As-Siddiq," the truthful one.
New beginning
622 CE — Year 1 Hijrah

The migration to Madinah

Facing growing danger in Mecca, he migrated to the city of Yathrib, later renamed Madinah. This journey, the Hijrah, marks the start of the Islamic calendar. There, he built the first mosque and united communities as brothers.

He and Abu Bakr hid for three days in the Cave of Thawr while being hunted. A spider's web and a bird's nest that appeared across the entrance are said to have convinced their pursuers no one was inside.
Turning point
Year 2 – 3 Hijrah

Badr and Uhud

The small, newly-formed Muslim community faced its first major test at the Battle of Badr and won against greater odds. A year later at Uhud, they faced a harder trial and learned lasting lessons in discipline.

At Badr, the Muslims were outnumbered roughly three to one, yet prevailed. At Uhud, archers leaving their post mid-battle turned a likely victory into a costly lesson about following instructions.
Strategy & peace
Year 5 – 6 Hijrah

The Trench and the Treaty

At the Battle of the Trench, Madinah withstood a siege from allied tribes using a defensive trench — a strategy new to Arabia. Soon after, the Treaty of Hudaybiyyah brought a rare, ten-year truce with Mecca.

The idea for the trench came from Salman al-Farisi, a Persian companion, who introduced a military technique the Arabian tribes had never faced before — and it worked.
Peace & forgiveness
Year 7 – 8 Hijrah

Reaching beyond Arabia

He sent letters to the rulers of neighbouring empires, inviting them to Islam. Then, in year 8, Mecca opened its gates to him — and rather than take revenge, he offered forgiveness to the city that had once driven him out.

He entered Mecca with his head bowed low in humility rather than triumph, and declared a general amnesty — even for people who had once persecuted him and his followers for years.
Farewell
Year 9 – 10 Hijrah

The Farewell Pilgrimage

Delegations from across Arabia came to pledge their support, in what became known as the Year of Delegations. In year 10, he performed his one and only Hajj, delivering the Farewell Sermon.

The Farewell Sermon spoke about racial equality, the rights of women, and the sanctity of life and property — principles still quoted and studied today, nearly 1,400 years later.
Legacy
632 CE — Year 11 Hijrah

His passing in Madinah

Prophet Muhammad ﷺ passed away in Madinah at the age of 63, in the home of his wife Aishah. He was buried where he passed away — a site now enclosed within the Prophet's Mosque, Masjid Nabawi.

His final words are remembered as an instruction to keep up the prayer and to be kind to those in one's care — a simple, lasting summary of the life he lived.
